Now, when you're given a brand new or existing, what's the first thing you do to understand
its current health?
I begin by analyzing how consumers perceive the brand today.
I look at brand equity scores, awareness levels, consideration, and repeat purchase behavior.
Then I review performance data such as market share, distribution, pricing, and competitor
activity.
I combine that with qualitative inputs from consumers, retailers, and digital sentiment.
This gives me a clear picture of where the brand stands and what gaps need to be addressed.
And when you identify a gap, how do you decide whether it requires communication, product
improvement, or something else?
I break it down into what I call the why, what, how framework.
Why is the gap happening?
What exactly is affecting consumer decisions?
How can the brand address it?
Sometimes the issue is functional, taste, packaging, format.
Other times, it's emotional, lack of connection, outdated positioning.
Once I understand the root cause, the solution becomes clear, whether it's product renovation,
a packaging upgrade, or a refreshed communication platform.
Let's talk execution.
Suppose you're planning a major campaign.
Walk me through how you manage that process.
First, I define the campaign objective, awareness, trial, consideration, or frequency.
Then I gather insight to understand what emotional hook will resonate.
I work with the agency to develop a strong idea that can translate across channels.
I ensure the messaging ladder is clear, the visual identity is aligned with the brand,
and the media mix is optimized for the target audience.
During execution, I monitor performance weekly, digital engagement, sales uplift, and feedback
from the field.
And finally, I evaluate the campaign's impact on the brand and document learnings for future
planning.
Can you share an example of a campaign that performed exceptionally well under your leadership?
Yes.
We were promoting a product in a highly competitive category.
Instead of traditional ads, we built the campaign around real consumer stories.
We worked with micro-influencers, created short-form content, and partnered with retailers for sampling.
The authentic storytelling angle created strong emotional appeal, engagement exceeded expectations,
and the campaign delivered a significant uplift in trial and penetration.
It reminded me that consumers respond strongly when communication feels relatable and human.
Interesting.
How do you manage innovation projects, especially when multiple stakeholders are involved?
For innovation to succeed, alignment is critical.
I start by building a solid case, consumer insight, market gap, feasibility, and business
potential.
Then, I work closely with R&D to test formulations with procurement to evaluate cost structures
and with supply chain to confirm production capability. Throughout the project, I keep
everyone updated with clear timelines and expectations. I've learned that innovation
fails not because of lack of ideas, but because of poor coordination. I make sure
everyone feels part of the process and understands the purpose behind the innovation.

Brand managers often balance long-term plans with short-term sales pressure.
How do you manage that?
I said clear KPIs for each.
Long-term KPIs include brand equity, penetration,
product roadmap, and strengthening positioning.
Short-term KPIs include quarterly sales numbers,
trade activation, availability, and tactical promotions.
I make sure that even short-term promotions
reflect the brand's strategic direction.
Tactical moves should support the long-term narrative,
not dilute it.
Tell me about a situation where you helped a struggling brand recover.
I once handled a variant that was losing share due to both taste issues and weak communication.
I ran retail audits, consumer groups, and blind taste tests.
The insight showed the product needed a better formulation and clearer value communication.
We improved the recipe, upgraded the packaging, and launched a digital first campaign focusing
on taste superiority.
Within six months, repeat purchases improved, distribution picked up, and the brand regained
lost share.
How do you decide when it's time to refresh packaging?
I look at consumer relevance, shelf standout, and functionality.
If consumers say the pack looks outdated, if it blends into the shelf too easily, or
if there are usability issues, it's time to refresh.
But I ensure recognizability stays intact, so loyal customers don't get confused.
A refresh should improve identity, not disrupt it.

becomes much smoother.
